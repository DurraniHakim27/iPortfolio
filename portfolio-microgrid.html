Solar PV System Design & Microgrid Power Management

From photovoltaic system simulation using SAM software to developing intelligent load shedding strategies for islanded microgrids — a comprehensive exploration of renewable energy systems.

Duration 6 Months
Topics PV Design & Microgrid Control
Software SAM, MATLAB/Simulink
Hardware ESP32, Solar, Wind
01 — Context

The Future of Distributed Energy

As the world transitions toward cleaner energy sources, two technologies stand at the forefront: Solar Photovoltaic (PV) systems for distributed generation and Microgrids for intelligent power management. This project explores both domains — designing and simulating a complete PV system using industry-standard software, then building a physical microgrid prototype with intelligent load shedding capabilities.

The motivation stems from a critical challenge in modern power systems: renewable energy sources are intermittent, and grid stability depends on balancing supply and demand in real-time. Microgrids — localized energy systems that can operate independently from the main grid — offer a solution by integrating renewable generation with smart control strategies.

This dual-focus project addresses both the design and management aspects of renewable energy systems, providing hands-on experience with everything from site assessment and financial modeling to embedded systems programming and power electronics.

Part I

Solar PV System Design & Simulation

Using the System Advisor Model (SAM) software developed by NREL, I designed a complete photovoltaic system from initial site assessment through financial analysis. SAM provides detailed performance predictions and economic calculations, making it the industry standard for PV system design.

Key Design Considerations

The design process involved multiple critical decisions: selecting appropriate PV modules and inverters, determining optimal array sizing based on energy requirements, configuring series/parallel string arrangements, and conducting shading analysis. Weather data from typical meteorological year (TMY) datasets provided realistic solar irradiance and temperature profiles.

Financial modeling in SAM included capital costs, operation and maintenance expenses, incentive programs, and electricity rate structures. This enabled calculation of key metrics like Levelized Cost of Energy (LCOE), Net Present Value (NPV), and payback period — essential for project feasibility assessment.

Part II

Microgrid: Power Management Systems

The second phase focused on developing a load shedding control system for an islanded microgrid. When renewable generation cannot meet demand, the system must intelligently interrupt non-essential loads to maintain grid stability and preserve power for critical services like hospitals, emergency services, and financial institutions.

Load shedding is a crucial practice for maintaining electrical grid stability — a controlled reduction of power consumption during high demand or disturbances prevents grid instability, equipment damage, and widespread blackouts.

The control strategy prioritizes loads based on criticality: Critical loads (hospitals, emergency services) receive uninterrupted power, Industrial loads are shed during low battery outside business hours, and Residential loads are shed during low battery during peak hours (9 AM — 5 PM).

03 — Hardware

System Components & Architecture

The physical microgrid prototype integrates multiple renewable energy sources, energy storage, power conversion electronics, and an intelligent control system built around the ESP32 microcontroller.

Complete hardware setup with ESP32, relays, and power converters

Key Components

  • ESP32 DevKit — Central microcontroller for data acquisition, decision-making, and relay control
  • Solar Panel (12W/6V) — Primary renewable energy source for daytime generation
  • Wind Generator (3-Phase) — Secondary renewable source providing power alongside solar
  • 18650 Battery Bank — Energy storage for maintaining supply during low generation periods
  • MPPT Solar Charger — Maximum Power Point Tracking for optimal solar energy harvesting
  • Buck/Boost Converters — DC-DC conversion for voltage matching between components
  • Inverter — DC to AC conversion for powering AC loads
  • Relay Switches — Controlled switches for load shedding implementation
  • Battery Gauge (MAX17043) — State of Charge (SOC) monitoring for control decisions

Power Flow

The wind generator produces 3-phase AC which is rectified to DC and stepped down via a buck converter to match the 3.7V battery voltage. Solar power passes through an MPPT charger for optimal charging. The battery stores excess energy and supplies power when renewable generation is insufficient. A boost converter steps voltage up to 12V DC, which the inverter converts to 12V AC for the loads.

04 — Control Logic

Intelligent Load Shedding Algorithm

The control algorithm monitors battery State of Charge (SOC) and simulated time to make intelligent shedding decisions. The ESP32 reads battery percentage from the MAX17043 fuel gauge, calculates the rate of change, and controls relays accordingly.

Real-time battery monitoring displayed on serial monitor
Blynk web dashboard showing battery percentage and change rate

Control Strategy: Above 50% battery — all loads powered. Between 26-50% — time-based shedding: shed industrial loads outside business hours (9 AM-5 PM), shed residential loads during business hours. Below 25% — emergency mode, shed all non-critical loads.

Remote Monitoring with Blynk 2.0

The system integrates with the Blynk IoT platform for real-time monitoring and alerts. The web dashboard displays battery percentage, change rate, and simulated time, while the mobile app provides notifications when load shedding events occur or when battery levels require maintenance.

05 — Results

Performance & Lessons Learned

The project successfully demonstrated a complete microgrid power management system with the following outcomes:

  • Accurate battery State of Charge monitoring with MAX17043 fuel gauge
  • Successful implementation of time-based load shedding strategy
  • Real-time remote monitoring via Blynk web and mobile dashboards
  • Automated alert notifications for maintenance and critical events
  • Stable operation of hybrid solar-wind generation system

Key Insights

This project reinforced the complexity of integrating renewable energy sources into practical power systems. The intermittent nature of solar and wind requires careful sizing of energy storage and intelligent load management. The load shedding approach — prioritizing critical infrastructure while maintaining grid stability — mirrors real-world utility practices but at a microgrid scale.

From the PV design perspective, using professional tools like SAM provided valuable experience with industry-standard workflows for site assessment, performance modeling, and financial analysis. Understanding how weather data, component specifications, and economic factors interact is essential for designing viable renewable energy projects.

The embedded systems and IoT integration demonstrated how modern microcontrollers can bridge hardware control with cloud-based monitoring, enabling sophisticated energy management strategies that were previously only available in large-scale utility systems.
