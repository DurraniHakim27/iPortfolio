The Goal

When we first started our project, the idea was simple but ambitious: create a robot that could move around on its own, sense its environment, and interact with it intelligently all by using only a single monocular camera. Unlike remote-controlled bots, this one needed to be autonomous, able to navigate toward objects as checkpoints, locate an industrial plant, and even monitor the plant temperature conditions in real time.

To achieve that, the robot had to answer two fundamental questions: what is around me and how far away is it. These are the same questions Tesla cars ask when they drive down the road, and we wanted to give our little robot that same kind of vision.

Depth Estimation: Seeing the World in 3D

We used a Raspberry Pi 5 paired with a Hailo-8 AI accelerator (26 TOPS) as ThermoVision’s brain. The Hailo chip was essential. Without it, YOLO ran at only 7 frames per second and SCDepth could not run at all. With the accelerator, both models ran smoothly in parallel.

  • YOLO detected objects: sphere, cylinder, cuboid, cone, and the “plant” (represented by a Maggi Hotcup).
  • SCDepth estimated the distance to those objects using a single camera frame.

The Flicker Problem

Here is the disadvantage of Ai depth estimation, the depth numbers flickered. A cone at 26cm could read 25cm the next frame and 29cm the frame after. This cause ThermoVision would inch forward, stop abruptly, then creep again. Watching it reminded me of someone learning to drive stick for the first time, lots of jerks and pauses.

The solution wasn’t just about running bigger models or collecting more data. The raw output needed stability. There is a simple but powerful idea from signal processing: Exponentially Weighted Moving Average (EWMA) filter. EWMA smoothed the depth readings, giving more weight to recent values. The effect was immediate. The numbers steadied, and ThermoVision’s movements became smooth and confident.

Blue line represents the flicker/jitter value of the depth estimation, while the orange line is the smoothed value by EWMA

From Vision to Navigation

With stable depth, the next challenge was navigation. ThermoVision used a simple but effective strategy:

  • When the robot is not detecting the target object, it will rotate clockwise or counter-clockwise to scan surroundings.
  • Once the desired target is in the frame, the robot will align itself so the detected object is at the center of the frame.
  • Move forward, adjusting with hysteresis to avoid oversteer.
  • Stop when distance is less than ~23 cm.

Plant Temperature Monitoring

The final checkpoint was the “plant.” ThermoVision used an MLX90614 infrared sensor to measure temperature without contact. The onboard ESP32 sent this data via MQTT to another ESP32 at the plant control station. If the temperature was above threshold, a cooling fan activated. Once below the threshold, the fan turned off and ThermoVision retraced its journey in reverse order.
